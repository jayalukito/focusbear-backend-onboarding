# Help Strategy

## When do you prefer using AI vs. searching Google?

I prefer using AI when I need an explanation, help understanding an error, or suggestions for different approaches to a problem. AI is especially useful when I am not sure what I should search for or when I want to understand why something is happening.

I prefer Google when I already know the specific error, library, or technology I am looking for. Google is useful for finding official documentation, GitHub issues, Stack Overflow discussions, and solutions from developers who have experienced the same problem.

AI responses should still be verified because they can sometimes contain outdated or incorrect information.

## How do you decide when to ask a colleague instead?

I would ask a colleague when the problem involves company-specific systems, business logic, architecture, or sensitive information that should not be shared with external tools.

I would also ask for help when an issue is urgent or when I have already spent a reasonable amount of time investigating the problem without making progress. Before asking, I would explain what I have already tried and provide relevant error messages or findings.

## What challenges do developers face when troubleshooting alone?

One challenge is getting stuck on the same assumption and repeatedly trying similar solutions. Developers may also spend too much time searching without understanding the root cause of the problem.

Another challenge is deciding whether an online or AI-generated solution is actually correct for the current project. Troubleshooting becomes easier when developers combine their own investigation with documentation, debugging tools, AI assistance, and help from teammates when necessary.
